Chapter 32

Nimue Breaks

The reports came in pieces.

Not the clean, organized pieces of Threadneedle's intelligence — the ragged pieces of a kingdom falling apart, each piece a fragment of a larger collapse, each fragment arriving at Wren's desk at a different hour from a different source with a different level of reliability, and the assembling of the fragments into a coherent picture was the work, and the work was exhausting, and the exhaustion was the condition.

Fragment one: Nimue's eastern army had splintered. The army that had besieged Thornhold — the army that had broken against the walls and the wards and Aldric's garrison — had retreated north, and the retreating had become fragmenting, and the fragmenting had become chaos. Three generals dead (Marcus's work, the work that had outlived the worker). Supply lines severed (Marcus's work again, the phantom units still consuming resources that real soldiers needed). Command structure collapsed (the natural consequence of removing the people who gave orders from a system that required orders to function).

Fragment two: Nimue's generals had turned on each other. From survival. The remaining generals, each commanding a fragment of the splintered army, had concluded — independently, simultaneously, with the brutal logic of military men who understood that a fragmenting army was a dying army — that the only way to survive was to consolidate. Consolidation required resources. Resources were scarce. Scarcity created competition. Competition created conflict. Conflict created war — not the war against Thornhold, but a new war, an internal war, a war of generals against generals, fragment against fragment, the army eating itself.

Fragment three: Nimue had retreated. The Witch of Despair had withdrawn from Greymarch — from her iron fortress, from her war council, from the machinery of power that she had built and maintained and commanded for centuries. She had retreated to a smaller fortress in the far north, a fortress that was not a seat of power but a refuge, a place where a woman who had built a war machine could watch the machine destroy itself and feel — what? Despair? The emotion she was named for? Or something else — something that was not despair but the thing beyond despair, the thing that happened when despair exhausted itself and left behind only the silence of a woman who had nothing left to feel desperate about.

Wren assembled the fragments. The assembling was the work — the cold, precise, strategic work of a mind that saw patterns in chaos and order in disorder and the specific, devastating logic of a kingdom's collapse in the scattered reports of couriers and spies and the occasional, unreliable testimony of refugees who had fled Greymarch and arrived at Thornhold's borders with stories that were too terrible to be believed and too consistent to be doubted.

The picture that emerged was — he searched for the word. The picture was "resolution." The northern threat was resolving — not through Wren's victory but through Nimue's entropy. The kingdom of Greymarch was collapsing not because Wren had defeated it but because Wren had weakened it enough for it to defeat itself, and the self-defeat was the most efficient outcome, because self-defeat required no additional expenditure of resources and no additional cost in lives and no additional weight on shoulders that were already carrying too much.

The efficiency was the thing that should have satisfied him. The efficiency was the system working — the system that he had built, the system of sabotage and espionage and the patient, meticulous destruction of an enemy from within. The system had worked. Marcus had worked. The forged letters had worked. The phantom units had worked. Everything had worked exactly as planned, and the working was the proof that the system was sound, and the soundness was the thing that justified the cost.

The cost was Marcus. The cost was three dead generals. The cost was an army of eunuch soldiers — men without feelings, men who had been made into weapons and were now being destroyed by the same system that had made them. The cost was Nimue — a woman who had built a kingdom and was watching it fall, and the watching was the thing that Wren recognized, because the watching was the thing he did every day, the watching of a system from inside the system, the watching that was both participation and observation, both action and helplessness.

He did not feel satisfied. He did not feel triumphant. He did not feel anything.

The nothing was the condition. The condition was the price.

* * *

Threadneedle's final report on Greymarch arrived on a Tuesday.

The northern kingdom is no longer a kingdom. It is a collection of territories, each controlled by a former general, each hostile to the others, each too small and too weak to threaten Thornhold. The threat is neutralized. Not eliminated — neutralized. Neutralized threats are dormant. Dormant threats can wake.

Nimue is in the northern fortress. She has not been seen in public. She has not issued orders. She has not communicated with her former generals or her former court or her former allies. She has — stopped. The stopping is not strategic. The stopping is — I use the word with precision — despair. The Witch of Despair has succumbed to her own nature. The irony is not lost on me. I suspect it is not lost on her.

The eunuch soldiers are — scattered. Some have joined the generals' factions. Some have wandered south, toward Thornhold's borders, seeking — I am not sure what they are seeking. Employment? Purpose? The thing that was taken from them? They arrive at the border in small groups, unarmed, silent, with the specific, unsettling blankness of men who have been emptied and have nowhere to put the emptiness.

Recommendation: the eunuch soldiers are a humanitarian concern, not a military one. They are not a threat. They are a consequence. The consequence requires management. I defer to your judgment on the nature of the management.

Wren read the report. Filed the report. Wrote the response.

The response was not strategic. The response was — he paused, pen over paper, and the pausing was the crack, the crack that had been growing since the garden, since the mead cellar, since Marcus's death, since Bess's flooding. The crack that let the human through.

The response was: Bring them in. The eunuch soldiers. Bring them to Thornhold. House them. Feed them. Find them work. They are not weapons. They are people. They were people before Nimue made them into weapons, and they are people now that the weapons have been discarded. The people deserve — the word is "dignity." The word is inadequate. But the word is what we have.

He sealed the response. Sent it. Sat at the desk.

The response was not strategic. The response was expensive — housing and feeding and employing a population of emotionless former soldiers was a cost that the kingdom's budget could not easily absorb. The response was risky — bringing former enemy soldiers into the kingdom was a security concern that Aldric would raise and that Wren would have to address. The response was, by every metric of the system that Wren had built, a bad decision.

The response was also right. The rightness was not the system's rightness. The rightness was a different kind — the kind that came from the crack, the kind that the systems could not process, the kind that was not data but feeling, and the feeling was the thing that the systems had been designed to suppress, and the suppressing was the thing that was killing him.

He had made a decision based on feeling. The feeling was — he searched for the word. The word was "compassion." It was in the vocabulary he was losing. Almost gone.

But not quite. Not yet. Still there, in the crack, in the space between the systems and the human, in the place where the warmth burned not for the kingdom but for the person underneath the kingdom.

The warmth burned — for the first time in months — not for the kingdom. The fuel was the crack. The fuel was the feeling. The fuel was the word "compassion," spoken quietly, in the privacy of a study at 3 AM, by a man who was learning — slowly, painfully, one crack at a time — that the systems were not the whole of him, and the whole of him was the thing that mattered, and the mattering was the thing that would save him or destroy him, and the saving and the destroying were the same choice, and the choice was the mirror, and the mirror was waiting.

The northern threat was neutralized. One witch-queen down. Two to go.

The war continued. The weight continued. The price continued.

But the crack was there. And the crack was growing. And the growing was the thing that the systems could not stop.

And the growing was hope.
