Author’s Note

This book began with a law: Nothing is created. Nothing is destroyed. Everything is transformed — and the transformation always costs more than you expect.

The alchemists of the medieval Islamic world called it al-mizan — the balance. They were scientists, philosophers, and mathematicians who understood that transformation was not magic but process, and that every process had a price.

Al-Mizan is a novel about that price. About the ash left after the fire. About the people who stand in the empty spaces left by transformation and ask: Was it worth it?

The characters are fictional. The alchemy is fictional, built on a foundation of real historical practice extrapolated into fantasy. But the law is real. The cost is real. The question, what are you willing to lose for what you need to gain? — is the realest question this story knows how to ask.

Asif M. Shah
